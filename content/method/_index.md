Old-time banjo is simpler to start than it looks — the core motion is a single
downward strike your hand learns quickly, then refines for a lifetime.

## The clawhammer strike

1. **Shape the hand.** Curl your fingers loosely as if holding a small apple.
   The striking finger (index or middle) stays firm; the wrist stays relaxed.
2. **Strike down.** Brush *down* across a melody string with the back of the
   fingernail. Never pick up.
3. **Sound the drone.** Immediately let the thumb catch the short fifth string.
4. **Find the rhythm.** The classic pattern is **"bum-ditty"**:
   *strike (bum) — strike + brush (dit) — thumb (ty)*.

## Common tunings

| Tuning | Notes (5th→1st) | Used for |
|--------|-----------------|----------|
| Standard / "G" | g D G B D | Most session tunes |
| Double C | g C G C D | Modal & "crooked" tunes |
| Sawmill / "G modal" | g D G C D | Dark, droning pieces |

## Where to go next

Start with two or three simple tunes, play them slowly until the bum-ditty is
automatic, then find other players. Old-time is social music — it lives in the
jam, not the practice room.
