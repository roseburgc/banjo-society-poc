## Our mission

The Old-Time Banjo Society is dedicated to preserving, documenting, and teaching
the traditional styles of banjo playing that grew up in the mountain and rural
communities of the American Southeast. We believe this music belongs to
everyone, and that the best way to preserve it is to keep it being *played*.

## A short history of the old-time banjo

The banjo's ancestors crossed the Atlantic from West Africa, where gourd-bodied,
skin-headed lutes had been played for centuries. In the 19th century the
instrument took the fretless, open-backed form that would become central to
Appalachian string-band music.

Unlike the three-finger "bluegrass" roll popularized in the 1940s, **old-time
banjo** is played in a downward-striking style — variously called *clawhammer*,
*frailing*, or *rapping* — in which the back of the fingernail strikes the melody
string while the thumb sounds the short fifth string as a rhythmic drone.

- **1830s–1860s:** the banjo enters popular American music.
- **1900s–1930s:** rural string bands flourish; the first field recordings are made.
- **1960s:** the folk revival rekindles interest in traditional styles.
- **Today:** a worldwide community of players keeps the tradition living and evolving.

## How we're organized

We are a volunteer-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit governed by a small board of
directors. Every dollar donated goes directly to workshops, our recording
archive, and instrument scholarships.
